# Readmission Exposure Analysis

**Seed question:** Which hospitals are most exposed to readmission penalties, and what's driving it?

## Situation
83% of hospitals with HRRP data (2,358 of 2,833) currently face readmission penalties, making this a near-universal financial exposure rather than an isolated problem. Excess readmission ratios range from 0.47 to 1.63 against a mean of 1.002, with hip-knee replacement cases driving the most severe outliers across 335 hospitals.

## Key findings
- 83% of HRRP-reporting hospitals (2,358 of 2,833) are subject to penalties
- Hip-knee replacement shows the highest average excess ratio at 1.217, above CABG (1.164), pneumonia (1.142), AMI (1.142), HF (1.133), and COPD (1.126)
- All 10 worst excess ratios (1.485-1.63) are hip-knee cases, spread across 7 states (OH, IL, MA, OK, FL, GA, NC) with no regional clustering
- For-profit and federal hospitals average 1.017 excess ratio vs. 0.995-0.999 for nonprofits and 0.953 for physician-owned, a 6-7 point spread
- Star ratings do not predict readmission risk: some 1-2 star hospitals score below 0.93, outperforming many 4-5 star facilities on this measure

## Recommendation
Launch a national hip-knee replacement readmission reduction program targeting the 335 affected hospitals, prioritizing the 10 facilities with ratios above 1.485 regardless of location. Use ownership type (for-profit, federal) and measure-specific excess ratios, not star ratings, to screen acquisition targets and partner facilities for penalty risk. Build measure-level dashboards per hospital, since aggregate quality scores mask condition-specific penalty exposure.
