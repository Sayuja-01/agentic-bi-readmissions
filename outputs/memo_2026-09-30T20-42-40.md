# Readmission Exposure Analysis

**Seed question:** Which hospitals are most exposed to readmission penalties, and what's driving it?

## Situation
HRRP penalty exposure across the 2,833-hospital dataset is not evenly distributed: it concentrates in a small tail of 77 hospitals (2.8%) sitting at the maximum penalty tier (>=1.10), while 1,339 hospitals cluster safely in the 0.90-0.99 range. The COO's question is which facilities drive system-wide penalty risk and what operational levers actually move the needle versus structural factors that don't.

## Key findings
- 77 hospitals (2.8% of 2,833) are at the max penalty tier (ERR >=1.10); 1,339 sit in the 0.90-0.99 safe zone
- Star rating is a strong proxy: excess readmission ratio falls from 1.048 (1-star) to 0.966 (5-star), an 8-point spread
- Worst performers (ERR 1.2-1.31) are single-specialty surgical/ortho hospitals measured on just 1 condition (e.g., Surgical Institute of Reading 1.31), yet other specialty hospitals like New England Baptist (0.594) rank best, so specialty status alone does not predict risk
- Ownership type explains little variance: severe-measure rates run 11.7% for-profit, 8.6% voluntary non-profit-private, 11.4% government-federal, with average ratios tightly clustered 0.953-1.017
- Geographic clustering exists: Colorado (1.242) and Oklahoma (1.237, 12 hospitals) show the highest average ERRs among penalized states; Massachusetts has the largest penalized cohort (27 hospitals) at a lower 1.185 average

## Recommendation
Direct intervention resources at the 77 hospitals in the maximum penalty tier first, prioritizing single-condition, low-volume surgical/orthopedic facilities rather than screening by ownership type, which shows negligible predictive value (0.953-1.017 range). Use star rating as a low-cost triage signal for broader portfolio monitoring given its consistent inverse correlation with ERR. Launch a targeted regional care-coordination review in Colorado and Oklahoma, where average ERRs (1.242 and 1.237) exceed even the largest penalized cohort state (Massachusetts at 1.185), to identify shared post-acute care gaps.
